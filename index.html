Do humans have to live with bed bugs forever?

BED BUG
EXTINCTION?

We are investigating the deliberate worldwide extinction of the bed bug species responsible for human infestations.

We do not know if it is possible. No present research result demonstrates a safe, selective route to that outcome.

THE CURRENT ANSWER: UNKNOWNWorldwide feasibility has not been demonstrated.Read the assessment →

We mean
extinction.

The literal disappearance of each target species worldwide. A clear apartment is relief. A clear city is a major public-health outcome. Neither is species extinction while fertile populations survive elsewhere.

The working target list is Cimex lectularius, C. hemipterus, and Leptocimex boueti. It must be revisited as taxonomy and host evidence improve.

Every independently sustaining population counts. That includes animal-associated populations. Bat-associated C. lectularius lineages have been documented; the status of other possible animal reservoirs is less certain. A human-only campaign would not satisfy the objective.

Why ask?

We are not researching better ways to live with bed bugs. We are researching whether humanity has to live with them at all.

Current responses are local and can require repeated effort when infestations persist or return. This project asks whether a permanent, species-level endpoint is scientifically and ethically possible. Its funding principle is equally explicit: affected households should not bear the cost of repeatedly solving a shared problem. No funding or free treatment service is established.

What failed the test

A project this ambitious has to reject its own attractive ideas when the required evidence is absent.

CURRENT VERDICT: RED

Contact is not enough.

Transfer between bed bugs has been observed in laboratory work. But the lasting number of viable daughters produced by surviving, secondarily exposed females has not been established. Even a strong effect would leave an untreated sustaining refuge alive.

Read the contact finding →
CURRENT VERDICT: RED

Inheritance is not a shortcut.

No self-sustaining suppressive system has been demonstrated in these species. A hypothetical inherited effect could spread only through populations that actually exchange reproduction; it cannot jump a disconnected lineage.

Read the inheritance finding →

One surviving source is enough.

The project's connected-population model puts a number on the problem. Under one deliberately illustrative scenario, a reached human patch declines while an untreated refuge grows. The connected system therefore does not meet the extinction condition.

Read the model limits →

What we still do not know

Where are all independently sustaining populations of the three target species?

Can low-density populations be found repeatedly in homes, farms, and animal roosts?

Could any intervention be safe, selective, lawful, and durable across every relevant population?

How would a worldwide absence claim be tested without mistaking nondetection for extinction?

About / Contact

Operation Itchless Someday is a research initiative investigating a literal global bed bug extinction objective. It is not an extermination service, an active eradication campaign, or a claim that an intervention already exists.

Any future proposal would need independent safety and ecological review, public governance, lawful access, protection for tenants and affected communities, and a financing model that does not place treatment costs on households.
