PROJECT RECORD
b45e201
TITLE

Modeling the last fertile founder and the unseen refuge

THE QUESTION

Under explicit assumptions, how do small-founder reproduction, movement into a fertile refuge, and repeated negative surveys affect the extinction question?

WHY IT MATTERED

The source reviews identified missing low-density establishment, movement, and detection measurements. A model can show which of those unknowns control an inference before anyone mistakes a laboratory egg count or a negative inspection for worldwide proof.

WHAT WE CHECKED

A new 13-stage branching process reconstructs the existing one-strain V1 mean matrix; an arbitrary-patch extension tests movement into an untreated fertile refuge; a beta-mixture observation model tests repeated negative visits with stable site heterogeneity and an optional inaccessible class. Six daughter-mating, four network, and three surveillance scenarios were run from explicit input JSON. An independent integer implementation ran 2,000 seeded founder simulations; 38 model tests passed.

WHAT WE FOUND

Under the Polanco 2011 C. lectularius laboratory life table and the model's lifelong mating-state simplification, the daughter-mating replacement boundary is 0.02864. With all daughters entering reproduction, one already mated unfed female has modeled eventual lineage nonextinction 0.8983; at an assumed 3% daughter-mating probability it is 0.0421. These are not natural-site establishment estimates. In an illustrative network, the reached patch is below replacement (daily λ=0.9510), yet the untreated refuge keeps the system above replacement; assumed 0.001 daily movement gives a seed in the reached patch modeled nonextinction 0.0268. With a hypothetical 10% occupancy prior and 20% mean visit detection, 30 negative visits leave 3.04% posterior occupancy under stable heterogeneity, versus 0.014% under near-homogeneous detection; an assumed 10% inaccessible class creates a 1.10% posterior floor. None of these mating, movement, detection, occupancy, or intervention inputs was fitted to low-density field data.

DECISION
YELLOW
WHAT THE DECISION MEANS

The overall worldwide-extinction assessment remains YELLOW. This model run does not measure field establishment or certify absence, and it does not reopen either RED architecture. F04 stays a live empirical-wall branch; F08 remains live with desk integration possible and field calibration missing.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER?

Observed failed as well as successful natural introductions, with mating state and multigeneration outcomes by species and host class; measured low-density movement and repeat-detection distributions including unreachable sites; and species-specific life histories for C. hemipterus and L. boueti could replace illustrative inputs. Even then, global reach and safety would remain separate gates.

EVIDENCE STATUS
MIXED — DESK WORK REMAINS BUT DECISIVE EVIDENCE IS EMPIRICAL
WHAT COMES NEXT?

Reconcile the existing low-density movement and survey records by stage, density, host setting, and method; then define ethical observational datasets that could identify founder failures and repeat-detection heterogeneity without deliberately seeding infestations.

EVIDENCE

  • Cooper et al. 2015 — apartment mark-recapture at larger infestation sizes ↗
  • Wang et al. 2016 — apartment inspection and interceptor detection ↗
  • Project research record b45e201 — model code, scenario inputs, results, and methods

PROJECT RECORD
46fba5f
TITLE

A shared nutritional weak point could not be decided at a desk

THE QUESTION

Do C. lectularius, C. hemipterus, and L. boueti share an indispensable, non-substitutable nutritional function across their sustaining natural populations?

WHY IT MATTERED

A shared, irreplaceable function could change the biological frontier, but marker detection or one laboratory phenotype cannot establish a worldwide, three-species vulnerability.

WHAT WE CHECKED

Primary loss-and-rescue experiments, digestive assays, comparative cimicid symbiont genomes, C. hemipterus regional marker and coinfection evidence, public metadata, and the published L. boueti voucher and assay record. The source-linked register separates detection, predicted capacity, functional necessity, rescue, alternative providers, natural diet, and assay limits by lineage.

WHAT WE FOUND

In C. lectularius laboratory work, dietary B vitamins restored nymph development, adult survival, and egg hatch to controls after verified Wolbachia loss; adult size and egg production recovered only partly. Riboflavin restored hemoglobin digestion in a later assay, but not all digestive deficits. These results contradict absolute irreplaceability of the measured vitamin contribution under those laboratory diets, not the possibility of a deeper shared function or the persistence consequences of natural diets. Comparative symbiont pathways are predictions, named symbionts turn over, and a single Wolbachia-marker negative does not prove nutritional independence. L. boueti was absent from the comparative genomic sample and has no verified functional nutrition result. No cross-species extinction-ready nutritional vulnerability was demonstrated.

DECISION
UNRESOLVABLE FROM EXISTING DESK EVIDENCE
WHAT THE DECISION MEANS

This decision applies only to the F03 hypothesis of one indispensable, non-substitutable nutritional function across all three target species and relevant lineages. It is neither confirmation nor falsification of every possible shared dependency. Literal worldwide extinction remains YELLOW; contact and heritable/autonomous architectures remain RED.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER?

For L. boueti, first verify a fresh specimen series from a demonstrably sustaining human-associated population and determine its partners and nutrient capacities; test a bat-site series if a sustaining bat population is established. For C. hemipterus, compare voucher-confirmed marker-negative and marker-positive, geographically and host-diverse populations for complete partner identity, nutrient status, natural diets, and lifetime viable daughters. For C. lectularius, measure whether laboratory dietary or partner substitution sustains lifetime viable daughters in human and bat-associated lineages on observed natural diets.

EVIDENCE STATUS
NEW EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE REQUIRED
WHAT COMES NEXT?

Close the desk audit while keeping the biological question open. If the residual function hypothesis is tested, begin with species-verified L. boueti material and baseline partner and nutrient capacity, then compare natural-diet nutrient provision and lifetime viable reproduction across lineages of all three targets under separate research and safety review.

EVIDENCE
  • Hickin et al. 2022 — controlled B-vitamin rescue ↗
  • Wiles et al. 2025 — riboflavin and digestion ↗
  • Yu et al. 2025 — cimicid symbiont comparison ↗
  • Tehran survey 2026 — C. hemipterus marker pattern ↗
  • Bernard 1970 — L. boueti type and dwelling record ↗
  • Project research record 46fba5f — shared-dependency decision, evidence audit, and register

TITLE

The frontier is mapped. The answer is still open.

THE QUESTION

After the mechanism and global-reach audits, which unanswered questions could still change the literal three-species extinction decision?

WHY IT MATTERED

A sequence of individual hypotheses did not show which routes remain informative, which can be tested from existing records, or where more literature review has reached its limit.

WHAT WE CHECKED

The frontier map compared biological leverage points and programme barriers across C. lectularius, C. hemipterus, and L. boueti; ranked twelve branches by decision value; and recorded each branch's desk yield and first empirical wall in a structured register.

WHAT WE FOUND

The map ranked twelve meaningful branches without demonstrating a mission-killing barrier or an evidenced three-species extinction route. F03 shared nutritional function was the highest-value remaining desk falsification test; F11 historical sources and F12 vouchers were partly desk-addressable. F04 founder success, F06 escape, F07 environment, and F08 movement allowed bounded reanalysis. F01 occupancy and repeat reach, F02 low-density detection, F04 field establishment, F05 sustaining host use, and full F12 taxonomy had empirical walls; any three-species F03 claim required L. boueti specimens and functional confirmation. The contact and heritable architectures stayed RED.

DECISION
YELLOW
WHAT THE DECISION MEANS

YELLOW applies to literal worldwide extinction across the three working targets. Contact and heritable/autonomous architectures remain RED; mapping a frontier is not proof of a viable mechanism.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER?

A source-backed universal impossibility or unavoidable safety veto could turn the overall gate RED. A genuinely shared, non-substitutable function would advance one biological hypothesis, but worldwide GREEN would still require safe selective delivery, reach of every sustaining class, and calibrated extinction certification.

EVIDENCE STATUS
MIXED — DESK WORK REMAINS BUT DECISIVE EVIDENCE IS EMPIRICAL
WHAT COMES NEXT?

Run the bounded F03 existing-data audit of a shared indispensable nutritional function, including published rescue, replacement-partner, host-lineage, and assay-limit evidence. Index historical and voucher sources for F11 and F12 where useful.

  • C. hemipterus survey 2026 — variable Wolbachia marker detection ↗

f8dd457
TITLE

The last refuge is still an open question

THE QUESTION

Is any independently sustaining target-species population inherently beyond repeated discovery, lawful access, and clearance?

WHY IT MATTERED

A single persistent fertile refuge defeats literal extinction. A truly undetectable or inaccessible sustaining class could make finite global clearance untenable.

WHAT WE CHECKED

Specimen-backed host records, human/bat population genetics, poultry observations, L. boueti primary records, geography and access registers, detection evidence, eradication analogues, and a last-refuge model.

WHAT WE FOUND

Bat-associated C. lectularius is the strongest nonhuman sustaining inference. Poultry-associated classes remain plausible or incompletely measured. One Ghana L. boueti specimen on a bat does not establish a sustaining bat reservoir. No reviewed source demonstrated an immense, permanently inaccessible occupied class; neither a complete population map nor repeat-detection performance exists.

DECISION
YELLOW
WHAT THE DECISION MEANS

YELLOW applies to literal three-species worldwide reach: feasibility and a fatal reach barrier are both unproved. The separate contact and heritable architecture decisions remain RED.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER?

A specimen-verified inventory of sustaining host classes, measured repeat discovery and access in the least observable class, and explicit accounting for unvisited habitat. Evidence of an effectively unreachable sustaining class could turn the gate RED; defensible global coverage could move it toward GREEN.

EVIDENCE STATUS
NEW EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE REQUIRED
WHAT COMES NEXT?

Pre-register a bounded, specimen-verified C. lectularius bat-roost surveillance feasibility study with repeat-detection measurement and explicit unsurveyed-habitat accounting; review L. boueti vouchers and localities in parallel.

  • Rosen et al. 1987 — poultry-barn C. hemipterus ↗
  • Nartey 2015 — L. boueti bat specimen ↗
  • Project research record f8dd457 — global reach decision and reservoir register

01a68a2
TITLE

Inheritance could not cross the missing links

THE QUESTION

Could a heritable or autonomous effect reach populations that contact exposure misses?

WHY IT MATTERED

Inheritance could reduce repeated delivery only where populations actually exchange reproduction. A disconnected fertile component remains a separate extinction obligation.

WHAT WE CHECKED

Target-species genomic and inheritance literature, natural symbiont observations, human/bat population genetics, mosquito analogues, and a conditional connected-population model.

WHAT WE FOUND

No self-sustaining suppressive inherited system is demonstrated in C. lectularius, C. hemipterus, or L. boueti. Sampled human and bat-associated C. lectularius are strongly differentiated; field exchange is limited or unmeasured. The model can suppress connected patches under hypothetical parameters, but its isolated fertile refuge survives every case.

DECISION
RED
WHAT THE DECISION MEANS

RED applies to heritable or autonomous suppression as the programme's worldwide solution on present evidence. It does not mean local inherited effects are impossible or that the overall extinction objective is RED.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER?

A demonstrated safe suppressive inherited system in a target species, measured field reproductive connectivity and resistance, and an independent way to cover disconnected components. No such program is established.

EVIDENCE STATUS
NO FURTHER WORK CURRENTLY PLANNED
WHAT COMES NEXT?

Record the RED decision in the mechanism ranking and close this architecture branch pending independent reservoir-closure evidence.

  • DeVries et al. 2020 — laboratory human/bat crosses ↗
  • Project research record 01a68a2 — inherited-suppression audit and model

f1de10b
TITLE

The survivor effect was not measured

THE QUESTION

Does a bed bug that survives secondary contact produce fewer viable reproductive daughters over its remaining life?

WHY IT MATTERED

Pooled egg counts and recipient mortality cannot prove lasting reproductive suppression among the survivors that would sustain a population.

WHAT WE CHECKED

Primary fungal-transfer studies, primary exposure and ingestion studies, an endpoint-level evidence register, and V2 sensitivity cases. The audit separated secondary recipients from primary exposure and mortality from survivor reproduction.

WHAT WE FOUND

No verified study tracked an individually identified secondary-exposed survivor through remaining-life viable daughter production. Aak 2018 did count pooled arena eggs; those eggs could not be assigned to surviving recipients. Hypothetical model breakpoints cannot supply the missing biological ratio, and even complete sterility among reached bugs leaves an untreated fertile refuge.

DECISION
RED
WHAT THE DECISION MEANS

RED applies to the contact-plus-lasting-reproductive-loss architecture as a worldwide route on current evidence. It does not decide the feasibility of literal extinction through every conceivable architecture.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER?

An independently audited study measuring remaining-life viable daughters per verified secondary-exposed surviving female against matched surviving controls, with mortality and recovery reported separately. That would test the biological effect, not worldwide reach.

EVIDENCE STATUS
CLOSED ON CURRENT EVIDENCE
WHAT COMES NEXT?

Ask a qualified bed bug entomologist to audit the endpoint register for any overlooked secondary-survivor lifetime viable-daughter dataset, using the outcome-only brief as the endpoint definition.

d7cf00f
TITLE

One untreated source broke the route

THE QUESTION

Can contact plus lasting reproductive loss extinguish a connected population if one fertile refuge receives no contact?

WHY IT MATTERED

A treated human population does not equal species extinction if an animal-associated or other source keeps reproducing, even without movement back to people.

WHAT WE CHECKED

A two-patch C. lectularius extension of V1; illustrative contact, kill, reproductive-loss, and movement rates; stochastic finite-population cases; transfer and reservoir literature.

WHAT WE FOUND

In one assumed case, the reached patch had daily λ = 0.92429, the untreated refuge λ = 1.05290, and the joint system λ ≈ 1.05195. Contact could drive a closed, fully reached model network below replacement, but no study established the required durable viable-offspring effect after secondary contact. Bat-associated C. lectularius also defeats a human-only endpoint.

DECISION
RED
WHAT THE DECISION MEANS

RED applies to contact plus lasting reproductive loss as a credible worldwide-extinction route on the evidence then available. It does not mean every possible route or the overall extinction objective is RED.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER?

Verify durable viable-offspring loss in surviving secondary recipients, calibrate adult and nymph reach, and show a credible path to every independently sustaining refuge. The model cannot manufacture these measurements.

EVIDENCE STATUS
CLOSED ON CURRENT EVIDENCE
WHAT COMES NEXT?

Write an outcome-only, preregisterable measurement brief for an independent contained C. lectularius study of secondary-contact incidence, mortality, and lifetime viable daughters among surviving contacted females.

EVIDENCE

  • Booth et al. 2015 — bat-associated lineages ↗

TITLE

Contact exposure faced a hard test

THE QUESTION

Could centrally delivered contact exposure spread far enough, and impair viable reproduction long enough, to avoid repeated household treatment?

WHY IT MATTERED

This route could, in theory, reduce dependence on repeated household participation. Literal extinction would still require every sustaining refuge to be included.

WHAT WE CHECKED

Primary studies on fungal secondary transfer and apartment movement; mechanism, resistance, species, and reservoir evidence; and published eradication analogues.

WHAT WE FOUND

Secondary transfer and mortality had been observed in C. lectularius laboratory settings. No study supplied the lasting viable-offspring effect after secondary contact, a calibrated daily reach rate, or coverage of all three species and nonhuman populations. The route was selected as a falsifiable research target, not accepted as an intervention.

DECISION
NO FORMAL DECISION
WHAT THE DECISION MEANS

The contact-plus-reproduction architecture was selected for a falsifiable test. This phase did not accept it as a working intervention or issue a formal color verdict.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER?

Measured adult and nymph secondary reach, individually tracked surviving-recipient viable daughters, and evidence that no fertile host or geographic component remains untouched.

EVIDENCE STATUS
EXISTING DATA ANALYSIS POSSIBLE
WHAT COMES NEXT?

Build a versioned three-species host-and-mechanism evidence register, then use it to set bounded ranges for the two-patch V2 threshold analysis.

  • Cooper et al. 2015 — movement among apartments ↗

TITLE

The question became global

THE QUESTION

What would the word extinction have to include beyond a treated home, city, or human-host population?

WHY IT MATTERED

A species survives if any independently fertile population survives. Local control or human-infestation elimination is a different endpoint.

WHAT WE CHECKED

The V1 model's boundaries, species and host records, apartment movement and detection studies, and the evidence needed to certify worldwide absence.

WHAT WE FOUND

The working target list became C. lectularius, C. hemipterus, and L. boueti, subject to taxonomic review. Animal-associated populations, travel, resistance, imperfect detection, and lawful access all sit outside V1. No present result established worldwide feasibility.

DECISION
NO FORMAL DECISION
WHAT THE DECISION MEANS

This phase defined the worldwide target and evidence gates. It did not issue a formal feasibility verdict for the full programme.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER?

A specimen-backed map of every sustaining host class, representative field vital rates, calibrated detection, and evidence that all populations can be reached and cleared.

EVIDENCE STATUS
MORE DESK RESEARCH POSSIBLE
WHAT COMES NEXT?

Build the taxon, reservoir, and detection evidence register before extending the V1 simulation.

6870327
TITLE

The first population model

THE QUESTION

Could a modeled common bed bug population fall below replacement when enough blood meals lead to mortality or reduced viable reproduction?

WHY IT MATTERED

A species-level endpoint first requires a way to make births fall below deaths. A closed model can test the arithmetic before any claim about field reach or safety.

WHAT WE CHECKED

A published laboratory life table for a Richmond C. lectularius strain; stage-structured deterministic thresholds; sensitivity checks; and seeded finite-population simulations.

WHAT WE FOUND

Under one illustrative combination of 40% treated-meal coverage, 80% attributable mortality per covered meal, and 50% viable-egg reduction among surviving exposed females, daily growth was λ = 0.9973. Stronger assumed coverage produced faster decline. These are hypothetical performance inputs, not measured delivery or a real drug effect.

DECISION
NO FORMAL DECISION
WHAT THE DECISION MEANS

This phase calculated conditional behavior for one closed C. lectularius model. It made no formal decision on worldwide feasibility or an intervention architecture.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER?

Measure actual meal-level coverage, joint mortality and viable-offspring effects, field life histories, and safety; then test immigration and other species. A modeled threshold alone cannot establish global feasibility.

EVIDENCE STATUS
EXISTING DATA ANALYSIS POSSIBLE
WHAT COMES NEXT?

No specific next action was recorded.
